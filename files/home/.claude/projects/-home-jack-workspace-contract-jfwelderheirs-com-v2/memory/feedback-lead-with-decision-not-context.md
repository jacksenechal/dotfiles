When asking Jack a question or presenting a decision, **lead with the one crisp question and let
context be pulled, not pushed.** Jack's observed anti-pattern in my output: I front-load several
paragraphs of complex context, he answers tersely on the one key point, then I unpack his terse
answer into even more verbosity. The decision point could usually be one concise question + a
terse answer, with the surrounding detail re-derivable on demand.

**Why:** over-emanation. Verbosity flows outward from a backing principle; most of the emanated
detail is re-derivable and therefore noise. Jack reads fast on short bullets, one thought per
sentence, and dislikes overexplaining (see global CLAUDE.md writing style).

**How to apply:** state the answer/recommendation first; put the question in one line; offer
context only if it changes the answer. Trust him to ask for the unpack if he wants it. Same
discipline as [[feedback-planning-tasks-need-strong-models]] deliverables: say the thing, don't
narrate the road to it. This is the communication dual of [[feedback-compression-pack-to-principle]].
